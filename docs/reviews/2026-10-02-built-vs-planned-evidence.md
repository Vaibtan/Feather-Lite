# Feather-Lite: built behavior, planned work, and the evidence behind the claims

2026-10-02. Read-only research review of the checked-out project, with one review artifact added. Code is the authority for current behavior; specifications describe intent; saved reports describe historical runs. This review makes no claim that historical provider calls, database integration tests, or deployments were reproduced today.

## Verdict

Feather-Lite is a substantial, single-tenant collections voice-agent prototype and its supporting control platform. Its unusual engineering depth is the separation of conversational reasoning, durable business decisions, and actual speech playout. The model can suggest a tool or an utterance; a deterministic control plane owns state transitions, protected-context access, validation, persistence, retries, and outcome recording. A LiveKit worker carries speech between that control plane and the caller. The repository also contains an operator console, scenario testing, post-call evaluation, observability, and three levels of load/conversation testing. This is much more than a prompt attached to STT and TTS. It is also not evidence of an enterprise production rollout, certified regulatory compliance, or large-scale live telephony capacity. [README](../../README.md), [PRD](../../PRD.md), [architecture decisions 0001](../adr/0001-conversation-loop-in-the-control-plane.md), [0003](../adr/0003-three-phase-turn-transaction.md), [0009](../adr/0009-scores-in-postgres-a-binary-judge-and-the-honesty-line.md).

The strongest defensible complexity story is **coordinating three different clocks**: a model can finish generating, a transaction can commit, and the corresponding spoken segment can finish playing at different times. An interrupted read-back, a delayed speech item, or a superseded turn must not be mistaken for an accepted promise. Current segment attribution is a contract between the control plane and worker, rather than an inference from whichever turn happens to be current. Identity gating adds a separate information-flow constraint: account details must remain unavailable before right-party confirmation. The prototype's right-party confirmation is borrower-name confirmation, not strong identity authentication. [Rebuild revision 2](../plans/2026-08-16-rebuild-plan-revision-2.md), [segment attribution spec, Phase 0](../plans/2026-09-05-attribution-contract-instruments-and-self-hosted-turn-taking-spec.md), [playout tests](../../apps/voice-worker/test/playoutAttribution.test.ts), [read-back guard](../../packages/control-plane/src/services/ToolExecutor.ts).

## Harness and metric audit

### What equivalence proves

Both [tier 1](../../apps/load-test/src/tier1.ts) and [voice equivalence](../../apps/voice-worker/src/tracer/equivalence.ts) compare the ordered `STATE_TRANSITION.to` list, ordered `TOOL_CALLED.name` list and final outcome with a passing scripted reference scenario. They do **not** compare all ledger events, tool arguments, call-control actions, speech wording or every information-flow invariant. The voice reference carries call-control information that the equivalence checker does not compare. This is useful business-spine equivalence, not complete behavioral equivalence.

Fleet exit success requires all calls equivalent, WER p95 within its configured gate and amount errors within budget. Latency, application SLO, compliance scores, unmatched transcript count, agent disconnect count and every TTS heuristic are not independent process-exit gates. Some silent cases fail outcome equivalence indirectly; that is weaker than a universal audio-quality gate. Tier-3 exit success is the scenario's expected-ledger verdict, with explicit expected-failure handling. [Fleet lines 373–447](../../apps/voice-worker/src/tracer/fake-borrower-fleet.ts), [scenario verdict logic](../../apps/voice-worker/src/tracer/scenarios-tier3.ts), [tier-3 runner](../../apps/voice-worker/src/tracer/sim-borrower.ts).

Scenario assertions themselves have limits: the hold scenario verifies a `wait` disposition, not that the agent stayed audibly silent for the entire window; read-back assertions require a recognizable read-back and tools in order; two declared persona scenarios are stubs. Expected failures are restricted to a matching failure pattern, so unrelated failures remain failures. A known-failure scenario passing unexpectedly deliberately requests removal of its stale marker. [Scenario registry](../../apps/voice-worker/src/tracer/scenarios-tier3.ts), [scenario tests](../../apps/voice-worker/test/scenariosTier3.test.ts).

### What speech and WER measurements prove

The borrower harness synthesizes fixed borrower lines, caches WAVs by provider/voice/text and publishes PCM audio through a real LiveKit microphone track. Known script text is the reference. Captured final STT fragments form the hypothesis; domain normalization plus edit-distance WER compares the two. That is real speech transport and STT evaluation against a known reference, but synthetic clean speech rather than a spontaneous human evaluation corpus. Date/amount normalization intentionally changes what counts as an error. Per-line WER quantiles/mean are not corpus-weighted WER. [Scripted call](../../apps/voice-worker/src/tracer/scripted-call.ts), [line cache](../../apps/voice-worker/src/tracer/line-cache.ts), [WER](../../packages/domain/src/wer.ts), [harness scores](../../apps/voice-worker/src/tracer/harness-scores.ts).

Transcript matching uses script timing windows; late finals can cross a line boundary, and unmatched fragments are reported but not independently gated. Score-to-turn joins use ledger `started_at` timestamps, a clock grace and bounded measurement windows rather than SSE turn IDs. The published `turn_latency_ms` uses first agent transcription delta after borrower playout, while audio energy is measured separately. Audio frames can contain silence, so receipt of frames alone does not prove audible speech. The degradation code's dropped frames are not equivalent to an independently validated PSTN packet-loss/jitter environment. [Scripted call](../../apps/voice-worker/src/tracer/scripted-call.ts), [join tests](../../apps/voice-worker/test/harness-scores.test.ts).

### Overlapping waterfall components: a current interpretation defect

In the installed pinned `@livekit/agents@1.6.4`, `dist/voice/audio_recognition.js:66–67` defines `transcriptionDelay = max(lastFinalTranscriptTime - lastSpeakingTime, 0)` and `endOfUtteranceDelay = max(now - lastSpeakingTime, 0)`. They share the same origin. At the EOU decision, EOU delay includes the elapsed wait for final transcript; transcription delay is not a separate serial stage to append to it. Current [Queries.ts:225–241](../../packages/control-plane/src/services/Queries.ts) sums EOU + transcription + TTFT + TTS into `total_ms`, and the [console waterfall](../../apps/console/src/views/latency.ts) displays them as stacked components. Therefore this derived total double-counts overlapping SDK timing and is not a clean end-to-end audio latency measurement. The saved SLO verdict remains what the application computed, but “2933 ms actual caller-perceived p95 latency” is unsupported. This conclusion is specific to the installed SDK version; later SDK changes must be re-baselined. The follow-up spec itself flags changed transcription anchoring on upgrade. [Phase 5.2](../plans/2026-09-05-attribution-contract-instruments-and-self-hosted-turn-taking-spec.md).

Tier-3's embedded `slo` block is also easy to misread: the runner queries a rolling production window of 50 calls; `harness IS NOT DISTINCT FROM null` excludes simulator calls. It is not the latency verdict of the scenario whose JSON contains it. Some compliance queries run before asynchronous evaluation completion, so absent scores must not be read as passing. [Tier-3 runner](../../apps/voice-worker/src/tracer/sim-borrower.ts), [latest report's `slo.segment` and `excluded_by_rule`](../loadtest/2026-09-05-tier3-backchannel-mid-line-resume-attributed.json), [aggregation](../../packages/control-plane/src/services/Queries.ts).

### Resource methodology and a remaining sampler defect

The sampler separates worker, server and harness processes, accumulates CPU counters, samples joint role memory, supports container counters and reports its basis. Host CPU budgets can use a busiest 60-second window; container budget fallback currently always uses the full measured window. The N=10 report explicitly marks `cores_over_full_window=true`. Script waits, staggered admissions, warm slots and bootstrap affect these ratios; mean whole-window CPU is not peak concurrent inference demand. Peak-minus-idle memory per configured call is an estimate, not a full density study. On Linux host sampling, the field called private memory is RSS rather than Windows-style private bytes. [Resource sampler](../../apps/load-test/src/resources.ts), [resource tests](../../apps/load-test/test/resources.test.ts).

There is a concrete current-code mismatch at [resources.ts:499–501](../../apps/load-test/src/resources.ts): `runningAmong(containers)` computes `present`, but `docker stats` is invoked with `...containers`, not `...present`. The preceding comment correctly says a missing requested container can fail the whole stats command, and this call preserves that failure condition. Thus later reports with missing container figures cannot inherit the N=10 resource conclusion just because they ran the same harness. Existing resource tests exercise calculations/validation, not this command construction. No implementation was changed in this review.

The default idle reading is the first sampler tick rather than a verified settled warm-pool state. The host measurement mark excludes setup CPU, while container cumulative counters begin at their first poll and may include setup outside the load denominator. Structural report validation checks field presence and declared bases, not the scientific validity or completeness of every measurement. ### Quality evidence boundaries

The deterministic evaluator checks rules and ledger evidence; the LLM judge supplies binary dimension scores with quotations and confidence. Neither is a calibrated human quality standard. Confidence is not used to abstain; agreement stays unavailable without human labels. TTS silence and chars/second are troubleshooting heuristics, not perceptual voice quality. WER ground truth exists for the harness, not normal production calls. A promise recorded is not a payment made; promise-kept requires an external payment feed. ## An honest answer to “What is the most complex agent you built?”

> The most complex agent I built was a collections voice agent with an independent transactional control plane. The interesting part was coordinating natural conversation with durable business actions: the model could propose a promise to pay, but the system had to verify the right party, validate the amount and date, play the exact read-back, and only record the promise after a valid confirmation with speech evidence.
>
> I separated model generation, database commit and audio playout. Each spoken segment had its own identity, so a late or interrupted read-back could not be attributed to the next turn. Turns were claimed and committed in short transactions, with model calls outside locks and ownership checks to discard stale decisions. Around that I built event-ledger replay, retries and scheduled callbacks, orphan recovery, a browser console, evaluation and a synthetic borrower harness over real WebRTC audio.
>
> The historical validation included ten concurrent voice calls reaching the expected business outcome and a separate 200-conversation scripted control-plane burst. It was a serious prototype, with remaining work in natural turn-taking, latency measurement and production hardening. The complexity was preserving correctness and information boundaries while conversation, transactions and speech proceeded asynchronously.

## Current implementation walkthrough

This section synthesizes the current implementation inspected in this audit. It describes repository capabilities; personal ownership should be narrowed to the components the interviewee actually built.

### What the product does

Feather-Lite is an outbound collections/payment-reminder voice-agent prototype with a durable workflow backend, an operator console, and an experimental voice-quality/performance lab. The implemented goal is to identify the right party, discuss account facts, obtain and record a confirmed promise to pay or a callback, and handle opt-outs, wrong-party contact, disputes, hardship, voicemail and silence. It records commitments; it does not charge a card, ingest settled payments or establish whether a promise was kept.

The current runtime is a TypeScript pnpm monorepo. Effect supplies typed services, boundary validation, errors, layers and scoped background work. PostgreSQL supplies durable records, row locks, ordered conversation events and queued jobs. LiveKit supplies rooms, WebRTC/media transport, job dispatch and the voice-session machinery. STT, LLM and TTS remain separate stages. This is one conversational agent with a separate optional post-call judge, rather than a team of autonomous planning agents.

| Component | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `packages/domain` | Pure state machine, overrides, tool schemas/eligibility, policy, context gate, replay, transcript, scoring and audio/turn-taking measurement functions |
| `packages/contracts` | Typed HTTP schemas and the SSE turn protocol shared by server, console and worker |
| `packages/control-plane` | Workflow and turn orchestration, PostgreSQL repositories, model adapter, tools, scheduling, finalization, quality and tracing |
| `apps/server` | Node HTTP composition root and scheduled-action, outbox and orphan-sweeper loops |
| `apps/voice-worker` | LiveKit agent/media adapter, remote turn streaming, speech-segment attribution, interruption/silence handling and voice harnesses |
| `apps/console` | Vite/vanilla TypeScript console for conversation review, simulation, live browser/SIP initiation, scenarios, quality and process status |
| `apps/load-test` | Scripted control-plane concurrency/soak tests and resource measurement |

A voice call's financial state lives in the control plane, not in LiveKit's conversational history. `FeatherAgent.llmNode` extracts the new borrower message and streams the remote control plane's result. A placeholder SDK LLM deliberately throws if the override is accidentally bypassed. This keeps simulation and speech on the same domain implementation. ### Follow one promise through the system

1. Starting a call locks the borrower, verifies contact linkage/validity and policy, checks contact hours and recent attempts, and prevents conflicting active calls or callbacks. It creates workflow/attempt/conversation records and initial events. Browser voice then gets a room, an agent dispatch and a participant token; SIP mode requires an outbound trunk.
2. The configured disclosure and right-party question are scripted. Financial context and protected history are withheld from model inputs until the right-party tool unlocks them in an allowed state. Verification is a verbal affirmative interpreted by the decider, not a separate authentication challenge.
3. A borrower says “I can pay 550 on Friday.” The decider receives the committed transcript, current state, relevant account facts and local date. It proposes `propose_promise_to_pay`. Amount and calendar date are schema-validated, but interpreting intent and relative dates remains model work in the OpenAI path.
4. The tool stores the pending amount/date and names the specific read-back segment. The state becomes `CONFIRMING_OUTCOME`. The deterministic read-back is emitted after the transaction commits.
5. The worker plays that segment and reports `AGENT_TURN_PLAYOUT` against its segment ID. A late SDK speech item is therefore attributed to its own segment rather than the newest turn. Zero-audio and interrupted read-backs cannot provide a successful voice acknowledgement.
6. On a subsequent affirmative, the model can request `record_promise_to_pay({confirmed: true})`. That call cannot supply a different amount/date. The executor uses the stored proposal and checks the read-back's own playout. Missing or interrupted voice evidence refuses recording and repeats the read-back.
7. An accepted confirmation updates the promise/outcome, finalizes the call and queues post-call work transactionally. Only then does the deterministic “I have recorded your promise” confirmation become available to the worker.
8. The worker drains the final speech before deleting the room. Post-call summary/evaluation and the optional judge run asynchronously.

The “heard” claim here is evidence reported by the SDK/worker about playout, not independent proof that a remote human perceived or understood every word. The guard checks segment acknowledgement, interruption and nonempty reported text; it does not re-transcribe the listener's received audio. Simulated calls deliberately bypass the missing-voice-acknowledgement requirement. See [workflow](../../packages/control-plane/src/services/Workflow.ts), [context gate](../../packages/domain/src/context.ts), [tool executor](../../packages/control-plane/src/services/ToolExecutor.ts), [deterministic scripts](../../packages/domain/src/scripts.ts), [segment ledger](../../apps/voice-worker/src/segment-ledger.ts) and [finalizer](../../packages/control-plane/src/services/CallFinalizer.ts).

### Why this agent is technically complex

The important difficulty is coordinating three timelines: model generation, database commit, and speech playout. They proceed at different speeds, can finish out of order, and can be interrupted independently.

The orchestrator uses a short claim transaction, a decision phase outside the SQL transaction, then a short validation/commit transaction. The final transaction verifies that this turn still owns the conversation before applying actions. A newer borrower turn can supersede an older model generation; the obsolete generation must not commit its tool result. Repeated turn IDs replay or attach, and repeated tool IDs reuse committed results. Keeping provider waits outside SQL row locks prevents model latency from becoming lock contention. See [orchestrator](../../packages/control-plane/src/services/Orchestrator.ts), [turn runner](../../packages/control-plane/src/http/TurnRunner.ts) and [concurrency tests](../../packages/control-plane/test/db/concurrency.test.ts).

The model proposes actions; code authorizes them. Twelve conversation states, eight domain tools, validated arguments and the state-scoped tool matrix constrain what can happen. Phrase-based overrides run before the model for opt-out, dispute, hardship and wrong-number cases. This is a useful division of authority, but it does not deterministically validate every free-form spoken sentence: ordinary model deltas can stream before the final commit; the strongest durable-before-speak guarantees apply to scripted tool confirmations. See [state machine](../../packages/domain/src/stateMachine.ts), [tools](../../packages/domain/src/tools.ts), [overrides](../../packages/domain/src/overrides.ts) and [OpenAI decider](../../packages/control-plane/src/llm/OpenAITurnDecider.ts).

The conversational behavior also includes actual barge-in, transcript-aware backchannel resume, a silent wait for an explicit hold request, a bounded hold for unacknowledged non-interruptible speech, and a two-strike no-input ladder. These behaviors do not establish a trained full-duplex conversational model. The fast backchannel resume uses a small lexicon and an SDK internal; semantic end-of-turn inference belongs to the installed LiveKit runtime. See [backchannels](../../packages/domain/src/backchannel.ts), [resume adapter](../../apps/voice-worker/src/resume-backchannel.ts), [silence clock](../../apps/voice-worker/src/feather-agent.ts), [wait policy](../../packages/domain/src/waitPolicy.ts) and [held turn handling](../../packages/control-plane/src/http/TurnRunner.ts).

Reliability goes beyond a successful dialogue: persisted callbacks/retries re-enter policy, leased jobs can be reclaimed after process death, heartbeats and room inspection distinguish an unserved call from an orphan, and readiness checks background loops as well as HTTP/SQL health. This is durable recovery of workflow state and failed-call cleanup, not seamless migration of an active audio call between workers. External media side effects remain outside PostgreSQL's atomic commit. See [scheduler](../../packages/control-plane/src/services/Scheduling.ts), [job repository](../../packages/control-plane/src/repos/scheduling.ts), [sweeper](../../packages/control-plane/src/services/Sweeper.ts), [process metrics](../../packages/control-plane/src/services/ProcessMetrics.ts) and [server loops](../../apps/server/src/main.ts).


### Documentation checks used for interpretation

This review uses the installed 1.6.4 source, rather than assuming the latest documentation's behavior matches the pinned SDK, for timing/ownership details.

The prompt's stable-first layout and per-state cache key are consistent with the principle in [OpenAI's prompt-caching documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching): reusable prefixes must remain stable, and `prompt_cache_key` helps route reusable prefixes on older models. The repository's measured cached-token counts establish historical reuse; they do not establish a latency benefit or justify carrying historical pricing into current deployment estimates.
